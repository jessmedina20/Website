Epilepsy care extends far beyond the clinic.

My research follows the practical, emotional, and informational work of living with epilepsy—from adolescence through later life.

Using large-scale analysis and close qualitative reading of epilepsy communities, I examine how adolescents, adults, older adults, and caregivers share experiential knowledge, interpret seizures, and negotiate safety, autonomy, and support.

Research perspective

Support is negotiated, not simply delivered.

Across my projects, people continually weigh the potential value of technology against practical effort, emotional safety, privacy, cost, accessibility, and the realities of care relationships. I use that tension as a design starting point rather than treating engagement as an individual failure.

What guides the work

  1. Center lived experience alongside clinical expertise.
  2. Design for changing roles, abilities, and support needs.
  3. Make accessibility and trust part of the core system.
