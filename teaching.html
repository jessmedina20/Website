Teaching

Learning happens through making, questioning, and revision.

I create classrooms where technical rigor and curiosity reinforce one another—and where students learn to see computing as a set of choices with human consequences.

Teaching philosophy

I teach by moving between concepts, concrete examples, and reflective practice. Students encounter an idea, test it on a meaningful problem, and then explain the limits of what they built. This rhythm helps learners develop durable intuition alongside technical skill.

Inclusive teaching is part of the course architecture: transparent expectations, multiple paths to participation, accessible materials, and examples drawn from varied domains. I use short feedback cycles so that confusion becomes information—not a verdict.
