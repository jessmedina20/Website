Research

Intelligence is never context-free.

My work investigates how computational systems understand people, communicate their reasoning, and affect the communities in which they operate.

A coherent program for human-centered AI

Machine learning systems increasingly shape decisions in education, health, work, and public life. Yet the assumptions encoded in those systems rarely match the complexity of the settings where they are used. My research connects technical development with human inquiry: I build new learning methods, study how people make sense of them, and evaluate their consequences in practice.

Questions I return to

  1. What context must a model represent to support a consequential decision?
  2. When does an explanation enable better action rather than merely greater trust?
  3. How should evaluation change when a system enters a living institution?
Project one

Situated Learning

Modeling the relationship between behavior, environment, and social context.

Problem

Behavioral models often flatten the contexts that produce their input data, leading to brittle predictions and misleading interpretations.

Motivation

Systems that account for context can better recognize uncertainty, variation, and the limits of transfer between populations.

Method

We combine representation learning, relational modeling, and qualitative fieldwork to encode and validate contextual structure.

Key result

Context-aware models improved out-of-distribution reliability while surfacing meaningful differences between settings.

Collaborators: Alex Rivera, Priya Shah · Human Systems Lab

Project two

Actionable Explanations

Designing explanations around the decisions people actually need to make.

Problem

Most explanation techniques optimize for model fidelity without measuring whether a person can use the explanation effectively.

Motivation

Interpretability should support informed judgment, contestation, and action—not simply confidence in automation.

Method

Participatory workshops identify information needs; controlled studies and field deployments test explanation formats.

Key result

Decision-specific explanations improved error detection without producing the overreliance seen with generic confidence scores.

Collaborators: Morgan Lee · Center for Responsible Computing

Project three

Sociotechnical Evaluation

Measuring impact across models, people, organizations, and time.

Problem

Benchmark performance often says little about who benefits, how practice changes, or where new risks emerge after deployment.

Motivation

Evaluation must capture the feedback loops and distributional effects that determine a system’s real-world value.

Method

We combine longitudinal field studies, algorithmic audits, and causal inference in a multi-level evaluation framework.

Key result

The framework reveals tradeoffs hidden by aggregate metrics and makes institutional assumptions explicit before deployment.

Collaborators: Jamie Chen, Taylor Brooks · Public Interest Technology Group
